### Religion, government, and cultural identity

This is one of the clearest decisions from our later campaign-planning conversations.

- Bridgehollow is not a Church of the Allfather settlement.
- Our November 2, 2026 canon correction established that Alvar founded the Church of the Allfather exclusively for the secluded Varrenmoor pilgrims.
- That church existed only in Varrenmoor. Bridgehollow has no Church of the Allfather.
- Bridgehollow operates under secularized, authoritarian Mwooreth law.
- Mwooreth survives there as civil and legal authority, not as religious worship.
- Heavy taxation, extensive regulation, and persecution of magic users characterize its governance.

This matters enormously for the town's architecture and storytelling. A municipal administration, tax office, courthouse, or enforcement building would be thematically consistent. However, their existence as specific mapped structures remains unconfirmed.

The same applies to customs: the governing culture is established, but individual ceremonies, punishments, holidays, clothing regulations, and everyday practices are not yet confirmed.

### Confirmed Bridgehollow-related encounter

## 3. Connections to the Ossuary Exchange and Ragged Mile

There is a crucial distinction between the party's journey toward Bridgehollow and the supernatural route to the Ossuary Exchange.

### Confirmed Ragged Mile mechanics

In Session 7, the party asks where the Ossuary Exchange can be found.

The response is:

> "Oh, that can be found at the end of the ragged mile."

Asked where the Ragged Mile begins:

> "At the intersection of three paths leading to any graveyards under a full moon."

The conversation establishes that the graveyards must physically exist and that the paths can be separated by some distance.

The October 4 handoff reinforces this arrangement.

The party's subsequent journey reaches the three-graveyard crossing and ultimately the Ossuary Exchange.

There is no verified requirement that travelers must enter Bridgehollow to reach the Ragged Mile.

### How the routes relate

Known narrative connections, not a geographic map:

- Varrenmoor → Pilgrim Road, 80–100 miles → Bridgehollow (intended destination)
- Three-graveyard crossing (reached during the party's travels) → Ragged Mile → Ossuary Exchange

These are narrative route relationships. The position of the three-graveyard crossing relative to Bridgehollow is not fixed by this diagram.

The Session 6–8 player chronicle places a possible Ragged Mile entrance roughly a day and a half farther along the party's route at the end of Session 8.

The three-graveyard site is associated with another ruined church and one of Bones's former comrades.

None of this establishes a physical entrance to the Ragged Mile within Bridgehollow.

### Bones and the Ossuary

Private Bones's remains were inventoried and sent to the Ossuary Exchange. This is confirmed in Session 7 and the player recap.

The Exchange is associated with bone accounting, ownership, provenance, and contractual obligations.

However, Bridgehollow's participation in that system is unknown.

We have also discussed an alternative character-background hook in which someone steals another person's identity in Bridgehollow, only to discover after that person's death that an obligation involving their bones survives them.

That idea is planned but unconfirmed, not evidence that Bridgehollow has an established bone-debt office, Ossuary portal, or municipal agreement with the Exchange.

It does provide a potential future connection between Bridgehollow's legal culture and the Exchange's contractual system.

## 6. Map-production recommendation

For a line-art town map that preserves campaign continuity, I would currently lock only the following:

- The settlement name Bridgehollow.
- Its relationship to Varrenmoor through the Pilgrim Road, approximately 80–100 miles and two days by horse.
- Its identity as a settlement governed by secular Mwooreth law.
- The presence of an authoritarian civil/legal system, including taxation, regulation, and persecution of magic users.
- A planned warden character, with identity and headquarters unresolved.
- Its relevance as a destination for the party and the transformed one-booted traveler.

I would leave the river, bridge, hollow, road alignment through town, settlement footprint, and individual buildings as unapproved cartographic decisions.
