# Building styles (2026-11-08)

Look-dev for the 3D world pack: timber frames at world scale, a preset system (25 styles), the Buildings tab in the Illustrator panel, Dyers Walk re-styled.
Images: `illustrator/out/building_styles/sheet.png` (all presets, same camera), `sample_<id>.png` / `<id>.png` (per style), `dyers/dw_walk.png`, `dw_aerial.png`, `dw_door.png`, `dw_street.png` (Dyers Walk pack),
`illustrator/out/foundry_screens/` (Foundry, if reachable, see the end).

## 1. Why the old frame looked huge, and what replaced it
`mat_wall_frame` was one texture repeat per 10 ft with the beam widths in image pixels: a 14 ft house wall showed one 0.5 ft post and one 20 ft diagonal. The structure was a texture, so no scale could be right.
Now the frame is **geometry**, generated per wall in world feet (`building_styles_geo.wall_frame`):
* beams 0.34-0.48 ft wide (4-6 in), 0.2 ft proud of the plaster (raised box beams; rails, studs, braces are real boxes, so the sun makes shadow lines);
* studs every 2.8-3.6 ft (braced / square panels) or 1.15 ft (close studding); rails at the floor line / plinth top, under and over the windows (2.55 and 7.5 ft), girts at storey lines, top plate under the eaves; panels 3-4.5 ft wide, 2-4.5 ft tall;
* diagonal braces lean toward the wall centre in about 60 % of free panels, only where the whole panel is free of openings; corner posts full height, extended to cover the corner notch;
* optional faux jetty (bressummer 0.55-0.6 ft proud with joist ends under it) between storeys; not a true overhanging upper floor (the shell is one extrusion, see limits);
* brick/stone plinth 1.0-2.6 ft as a raised course (0.14 ft proud), door and window clear; leaded/casement/sash bars, shutters, chimneys (brick stack on the gable end or the ridge), ridge caps, barge boards, thick thatch edges, crow-stepped gables, towers with spires, stacked stave roofs, windmill tower and sails.
* No coplanar overlaps: boxes skip the face against the wall, studs/braces skip faces that abut rails, beams subtract the (expanded) opening rectangles, the top plate stays under the roof surface (roof height at the beam's outward position).
Triangles: a styled two-storey Tudor house is about 3 k triangles (Dyers Walk pack: 81 k triangles total).

## 2. Preset system
* Files: `illustrator/presets/building_styles/<id>.json` (one per style; fields `name, description, category, culture, kinds{kind: weight}, wall, plinth, roof, windows, door, chimney, trim_colors, special, tile_scale, preview`). Lists = pick deterministically per building id (colour, beam colour, pattern, spacing, pitch, overhang, window grid, door colour...), `[lo, hi]` = value in range.
* Code: `generators/building_styles.py` (load, resolve, default_style, apply_style, dynamic materials + textures, CLI `list|summary|assign|resolve`), `building_styles_geo.py` (geometry), `building_styles_preview.py` + `foundry-3d/blender/style_preview.py` (sheet).
* Building json: `"style": "<preset id>"` (or `"auto"` = chosen from kind + district by `default_style`, 65 % of a district's buildings share its preferred look). **No `style` key = exactly the old look** (`apply_style` returns the same entity; tested).
  `worldpack.export_world` carries `style` into the pack entity; `worldpack_mesh.build_parts` applies it after `restyle_building`.
* Materials: ids `mat_s_<kind>_<rrggbb>` (plaster, brick, board, clap, log, beam, turf, flat) and `mat_s_tint_<base>_<rrggbb>` (existing roof/door/trim/stone textures tinted to a colour). Records come from `building_styles.material_record`, so merge/compress, which loads the pack without building meshes, still resolves them (`write_glb` falls back to it); textures are generated by `worldpack_pbr.install` (512 px, albedo + normal + for tile/slate metallicRoughness). Tile sizes: brick 4 ft per repeat (16 courses = 3 in), boards 4-4.5 ft, plaster 6 ft, roofs 9 ft (thatch styles 5.4 ft: `tile_scale` 0.6).
* Styles (25): houses `tudor`, `danish_fachwerk`, `falu_cottage`, `hanseatic_brick`, `niedersachsen_halle`, `danish_longhouse`, `norse_log`, `tarred_timber`; town `town_inn`, `town_shop`, `town_smithy`, `town_warehouse`, `town_mill`, `town_barn`, `town_guildhall`, `town_watchhouse`, `town_gatehouse`; churches `chapel`, `parish_church`, `stave_church`; other `boathouse`, `well_house`, `windmill`, `watermill`, `farm_longhouse`.

## 6. Limits / honest notes
* Jetty is faux (projecting bressummer + joist ends, wall plane unchanged). Gable timber is built at the obb end plane; a skewed (non-rectangular) footprint will not line up exactly, as before with gable triangles.
* Window positions still come from `worldpack_detail` (every ~8 ft, 3.5 x 7 ft doors); styles can now reshape them (section 7). The gatehouse has only a round-arched pedestrian door, no carriage archway; the watermill has no wheel, the windmill sails are flat slat panels. Dormers, porches, shop signs, open boat-house doors are not modelled.
* Colour is per preset palette; Blender previews use the Standard view transform, Foundry's lighting will read the same colours slightly differently.
* Thatch/tile/slate roof textures are the older images, only tinted (strands look soft at 2 m).
* Stone plinth/stone walls use the rubble texture at 7 ft per repeat (blocks about 1 x 2 ft).

## 7. Arched openings (building_styles_arch.py)
Preset schema additions (all optional, default = the old rectangular look):
* `windows.shape`: `rect` | `round_arch` (semicircle) | `pointed` (equilateral two-centred gothic lancet) | `rose` (circle in the wall) | `triple_lancet` (three pointed lights, centre one taller, stone piers between). A list picks per building like the other fields.
  `windows.width_ft` (1.9), `height_ft` (6.5, total with the arch), `sill_ft` (derived windows only; interior layout windows keep their sill), `triple_width_ft` (4.8).
* `door.shape`: `rect` | `round_arch` | `pointed`: arched hole, plank leaf cut to the arch (still ONE material per door node, hinge unchanged), stone surround.
* `special.rose`: `{diameter_ft, end}`: round window on a gable (default the end opposite a tower): stone ring with alternating proud voussoirs, glass, 8 spokes + hub ring.
Geometry: the wall still cuts the bounding rectangle of the hole; the spandrels (corners) are filled on both wall faces, so the opening seen from outside IS the arch; curved soffit and jambs inside, stone jambs + voussoir ring outside, arched glass pane, tracery (mullion, saddle bars, inner arch ring). Windows stay `kind: window` holes (material `mat_glass`) so the split exporter and interiors see the same hole list; windows only narrow inside the span they had (partitions still clear), and stay under the storey line.
Applied: `parish_church` (pointed / triple lancets, pointed door, rose in the gable opposite the tower), `chapel` (round-arched windows, pointed door), `stave_church` (small round-arched windows, round portal), `town_gatehouse` and `town_guildhall` (round-arched door).
Images: `building_styles/churches.png`, `sheet.png`. Tests: `ArchedOpenings` in test_building_styles.py (16 tests in all).
Limits: the gable rose is blind (a ring and glass disc proud of the gable face; the gable triangle is not cut); a rose window in a wall hole (`windows.shape: rose`) works but no preset uses it; no trefoil; the door surround is the style's trim colour (stone on churches); doors keep the width from the region detail pass (3.5 ft); the Dyers Walk pack uses no church style, so it was not regenerated.

Tower fix: the stave tower is one shaft (4 corner posts floor to plate, board walls from the plate up through the roof, roof sheet clipped inside the footprint by building_styles_geo.clip_roof, flashing collar, ridge cap and barge stop at the walls, stepped skirt roofs between tiers, spire). The parish tower roof is cut the same way (its walls already run from the footing). Interiors keep furniture out of the stave tower bay. Images: building_styles/stave_fix_before.png, stave_fix_after.png (3/4, low, section). Test: Geometry.test_tower_walls_run_through_the_roof.
